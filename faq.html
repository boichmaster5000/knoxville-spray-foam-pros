Safety and Air Quality

Once fully cured, spray foam is considered relatively inert, according to EPA. The exposure concern is during spraying and curing, which is why occupants leave and installers wear supplied-air respirators. Cutting, grinding or heating cured foam later can release emissions again, so anyone doing that work should ventilate and protect their lungs.

A smell that lingers well past the first few days is not normal. It can point to foam that was mixed off ratio or sprayed too thick in one pass. The Consumer Product Safety Commission advises contacting the installer and asking for the product's safety data sheet. If the foam itself is the problem, removal may be the fix.

Canned foam is fine for sealing small gaps around pipes and wires. Large two-part jobs are a different matter. EPA says indoor two-part spraying typically calls for a full-face or hooded supplied-air respirator, because ventilation alone does not protect the person spraying. Getting the mix, temperature and pass thickness right also takes equipment most homeowners do not have.

Spray foam is a plastic, so it will burn if exposed to enough heat. That is why building codes require foam in living spaces to sit behind a thermal barrier such as half-inch drywall, and why attics and crawl spaces often need an ignition barrier or an approved coating. Foams are tested for flame spread, and we plan the right covering for each area.

Not during two-part spray foam work. EPA guidance says occupants should be out during application and that re-entry decisions should account for children, who spend time near floors and dust. Plan for pets the same way. Fiberglass and blown-in jobs are less disruptive, and we will tell you at the estimate what your job requires.

Your House After Insulation

Roofers can replace shingles over a foamed deck, but tell them it is there. The Department of Energy guide points out that a leak will not show through closed cell foam on the deck, so it can go unnoticed longer. Ask your roofer and shingle manufacturer how they treat under-deck foam before you schedule either job.

It can if foam covers the sill plate and the top of the foundation. Department of Energy guidance calls for leaving an inspection gap where termites are a concern, and pest control companies in the Southeast want to see that wood. Tell us if you have a termite contract, and check with that company before any foam goes on foundation walls.

A tighter house holds onto moisture and combustion gases that used to leak out. The Department of Energy recommends confirming fresh air ventilation and the venting of gas appliances before tightening a home. If you have a gas water heater or furnace inside the area we are sealing, we will point it out so your HVAC contractor can check it.

Turn off the breaker, remove an outlet cover on an exterior wall, and look along the edge of the box with a flashlight. You can also check where a pipe or cable enters a wall in the basement or crawl space. Cold walls in winter are another clue. At a free estimate we check several walls before recommending injection foam.

Not right away, but keep it in mind for the next replacement. Insulation and air sealing reduce how much heating and cooling the house needs. When your system is due for replacement, ask the HVAC contractor for a proper load calculation on the improved house instead of matching the old unit's size.

Getting Ready for Install Day

Clear a path to the attic hatch or crawl space door, move stored boxes out of the work area, and park vehicles away from the garage if we are working there. For spray foam jobs, arrange to be away with kids and pets. We cover floors and furnishings near the work area and go over the details when we schedule.
