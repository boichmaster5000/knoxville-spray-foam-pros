What does spray foam cost in Knoxville?

National 2026 cost guides put installed spray foam at roughly $1.50 to $5.00 per square foot. Open cell sits near the low end and closed cell near the high end, because closed cell uses more chemical per inch. Thickness, access and prep move the price more than anything else. Your free estimate gives the real number.

Open cell or closed cell?

Open cell is soft, lighter and cheaper, at about R-3.6 to R-3.8 per inch, and it lets moisture pass. Closed cell is dense and rigid, at about R-6 to R-7.4 per inch, and one inch already slows vapor. Open cell suits most attics and interior walls. Closed cell belongs on crawl space walls, rim joists and metal.

Can you insulate walls that are already finished?

Yes. Injection foam is pumped into closed stud cavities through small holes, usually from outside behind a lifted course of siding or through brick mortar joints. The holes are plugged and patched afterward. One widely used injection foam rates about R-4.6 per inch, so an empty 2x4 wall gains real insulation without new drywall.

Why Insulation Works Harder in East Tennessee

Knoxville sits in Climate Zone 4A, the mixed and humid band of the energy code map. A home here has to hold heat through winter nights and shed moisture through long, sticky summers. NOAA's 1991 to 2020 normals for McGhee Tyson Airport show about 3,528 heating degree days and 1,574 cooling degree days a year, along with almost 52 inches of rain.

Heat is only half of it. Humid outdoor air carries moisture into every gap it finds, and the cold surfaces it meets are where problems start: crawl space joists, the band joist above the foundation, the underside of a metal roof. Spray foam does two jobs at once in those spots. It insulates, and it seals the air path so the damp air never reaches the cold surface.

Crawl spaces deserve their own mention. Many houses in Knox, Blount and Anderson counties sit on vented crawls, and Department of Energy research found that in humid climates the outdoor air pulled in through those vents is often wetter than the crawl space itself. Its Building America work measured 15 to 18 percent lower heating and cooling energy in sealed, conditioned crawl spaces compared with vented ones.

Tennessee's statewide energy code still uses the older 2009 insulation table, so the prescriptive minimum for a Zone 4 ceiling is R-38 and for a framed wall is R-13. ENERGY STAR recommends more: R-49 to R-60 in a Zone 4A attic, depending on what is already up there. Many older Knoxville homes fall well short of either number, and plenty of 1950s and 1960s houses have walls with nothing in them at all.

The payoff is real but worth stating honestly. ENERGY STAR estimates that air sealing plus added insulation in attics, floors over crawl spaces and basement rim joists saves homeowners an average of 15 percent on heating and cooling, or about 11 percent on total energy costs.
