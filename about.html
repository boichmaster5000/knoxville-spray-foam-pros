About Knoxville Spray Foam Pros

We are a local insulation company in Knoxville, Tennessee. Insulation is all we do: no remodeling, no roofing, no side trades. Call or send the form and we will come out, look at the job and give you a free estimate.

What We Do

Our main trade is spray foam, both open cell and closed cell, applied to attics, roof decks, crawl space walls, rim joists, framed walls and metal buildings. Our specialty is injection foam for existing homes, which fills closed exterior walls through small holes so older houses can be insulated without tearing out plaster or drywall.

We also install fiberglass batts and blown-in fiberglass and cellulose, put down crawl space vapor barriers, handle air sealing, and remove old or failed insulation. Spray foam is not always the best value, and we would rather recommend batts or blown-in where they make sense than sell foam everywhere.

How We Work

  • We look before we price. Every quote starts with a free estimate at the property. We measure, check the existing insulation, look for moisture and air leaks, and explain what we see.
  • We put it in writing. You get a written price that lists the product, the thickness or R-value, the areas covered, and any removal or prep.
  • We explain the tradeoffs. Open cell or closed cell, foam or blown-in, roof deck or attic floor: each has a cost and a reason, and we walk you through both.
  • We clean up. Trimmings and debris leave with us, holes from injection foam are plugged and patched, and we walk the finished work with you.

Why East Tennessee Houses Need Different Thinking

Knoxville and the surrounding counties sit in Climate Zone 4A, where homes need heat in winter and moisture control all summer. That shapes how we insulate: crawl spaces and band joists get as much attention as attics, metal buildings get closed cell foam bonded to the steel, and we pay attention to where water vapor will go before choosing a product.

The housing stock varies just as much. In Oak Ridge, Census figures show nearly a third of homes date to the 1940s, when the city was built for the Manhattan Project. In Farragut, the median home was built in 1992, so the questions there are about big attics and long duct runs rather than empty walls. Each house calls for a different plan, which is why we start with a visit instead of a price list.
