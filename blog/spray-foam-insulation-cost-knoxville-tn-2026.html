Spray Foam Insulation Cost in Knoxville TN: Complete 2026 Pricing Guide

Updated May 2026. Pricing reflects current Knoxville-area material and labor cost.

Why Two Quotes for the Same House Differ

Revised October 2026. Every cost figure below was checked that month.

Two spray foam quotes for one Knoxville house usually differ because they price two different jobs. One crew plans 3.5 inches of open cell on the roof deck and the other plans 5.5. One includes pulling the old attic floor insulation, a fire coating and haul-off, and the other leaves those out or quotes chemical only. Put both into the same terms, inches, R-value, board feet and a list of what is included, and most of the gap explains itself.

This article shows how to do that. For the current national price ranges by job type, use our Knoxville spray foam cost guide.

Board Feet Math on a Real Example

A board foot is one square foot of foam at one inch deep. HomeBlue's Knoxville cost page works an example of 1,000 square feet at 3 inches, which is 3,000 board feet. Spray that same 1,000 square feet at 5.5 inches and you get 5,500 board feet, roughly 83 percent more foam.

Run both through Angi's 2026 installed range for open cell, $0.60 to $1.60 per board foot. The 3 inch job comes to $1,800 to $4,800. The 5.5 inch job comes to $3,300 to $8,800. Same area, same foam, and two fair quotes can sit thousands of dollars apart on depth alone.

When a quote gives a total and a board foot count, divide one by the other. That unit price is the only number you can hold up against another installer or against a published range. If a quote is written per square foot, ask for the board feet anyway.

Thickness and R-Value Traps

An R-value that does not fit the inches. Open cell gives about R-3.5 to R-4.0 per inch, so 3.5 inches works out to roughly R-12 to R-14. A quote promising much more at that depth owes you an explanation.

Average depth instead of minimum. Foam rises in uneven waves. Ask whether the stated inches are a minimum and how the crew will check them once the foam has cured.

"Meets code" with no explanation. Tennessee's statewide code sets R-38 for a Zone 4 ceiling, and Maryville moved to the 2024 IECC on January 1, 2026. At R-3.5 to R-4.0 per inch, open cell needs about 9.5 to 11 inches to reach R-38 by depth alone, so a thinner roof deck foam job has to show compliance another way, such as a whole-house U-factor calculation. Ask which path the quote uses, and confirm it with your local building office.

A hybrid roof with too little foam. When batts or other air-permeable insulation go under a roof deck, IRC Table R806.5 calls for at least R-15 of air-impermeable insulation against the deck in Zone 4A. That is about 2 to 2.5 inches of closed cell. One inch of foam under fiberglass does not meet it.

Thick closed cell in one shot. One closed cell data sheet allows 1 to 2 inches per pass, or a single pass up to 3 inches, and the foam must cool below 100 F before the next pass. A thick closed cell job takes several passes and real crew time.

Open Cell vs Closed Cell: Compare Price per R

If one quote uses open cell and the other closed cell, comparing price per square foot is misleading. This Old House lists 1 inch of open cell at $0.44 to $1.50 per square foot and 1 inch of closed cell at $1.25 to $2.90, about two to three times as much. EPA describes open cell at roughly R-3.5 per inch and closed cell at roughly R-6.5. Divide, and open cell costs about $0.13 to $0.43 per R per square foot while closed cell costs about $0.19 to $0.45. DOE's guide puts the gap at roughly 60 percent for equal R-value.

So the gap per R is smaller than it looks, and the real question is what each foam does. One closed cell data sheet shows 0.91 perm at 1 inch, a Class II vapor retarder, and DOE marks open cell as not acceptable below grade. That is why closed cell belongs on crawl walls, rim joists and metal, and why open cell foam is the usual value choice under a roof deck. Two quotes that disagree on foam may simply disagree on design. Our closed cell foam page covers where the premium pays.

Prep and Removal

Prep is where quotes quietly split. Fixr puts insulation removal at $1.50 to $3 per square foot by machine and about $5 where it has to be done by hand. HomeAdvisor adds $293 to $875 when drywall must come off to reach a cavity. One quote may carry those lines and the other may assume you will clear the attic yourself.

Moisture matters too. Knoxville averages almost 52 inches of rain a year, and plenty of crawl spaces here stay damp. One closed cell data sheet requires the surface to be at least 5 F above the dew point before spraying, so a wet crawl may need drying before foam goes on. A quote that skips that step is cheaper only on paper.

Coatings and Fire Barriers

In an attic or crawl space entered only for service, code lists coverings that can serve as an ignition barrier over foam, such as 1.5 inches of mineral fiber, 1/4 inch wood structural panel or 3/8 inch gypsum. Some foams use a sprayed coating instead. One open cell data sheet calls for a listed intumescent coating of 3 to 10 mils dry in attics, while one closed cell product passed testing that lets it go without the prescriptive barrier there.

Living space is stricter. Foam there needs a thermal barrier such as 1/2 inch drywall, and one manufacturer's attic guide says an attic used for storage needs the foam itself covered. If one quote has a coating line and the other does not, ask whether the second product truly needs none in that spot or whether the coating was simply left out.

What a Low Quote May Leave Out

  • Fewer inches than the other bid, with no R-value stated.
  • Gable ends, knee walls and the attic hatch.
  • Removal and disposal of old insulation.
  • The ignition barrier coating.
  • Combustion appliances. One manufacturer's guide says fuel-burning appliances in an unvented attic must be sealed combustion or power vented, so an older furnace or water heater up there may need HVAC work first.
  • Crawl space drying. A sealed crawl under IRC R408.3 needs a Class I vapor retarder plus one drying method, such as exhaust, conditioned air or a dehumidifier sized at 70 pints per day per 1,000 square feet of floor.
  • Labor. Fixr's $0.44 to $0.65 open cell board foot figure is material only.

Questions to Ask Every Installer

  1. What is the minimum depth in each area, and what R-value does that give?
  2. Open cell or closed cell, and at what density?
  3. How many board feet does this price assume?
  4. What comes out before you spray, and who hauls it away?
  5. Does this product need an ignition or thermal barrier where it is going, and is that included?
  6. How will you check the finished depth?
  7. What does this quote not cover?
  8. Are you a member of TVA's Quality Contractor Network, if I want the rebate?

Reading Quotes: Common Questions

Until then, a low number often just means less foam, no removal or no coating. Ask the low bidder to price the missing items so you can compare totals fairly.
