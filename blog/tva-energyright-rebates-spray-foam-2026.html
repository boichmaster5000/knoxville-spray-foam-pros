TVA EnergyRight Insulation Rebates in 2026: What Knoxville Homeowners Can Claim

In 2026, Knoxville homeowners can claim TVA EnergyRight rebates of $300 each for attic insulation, wall insulation, envelope air sealing, and duct sealing and insulation, as long as a Quality Contractor Network member does the work. Income-qualified KUB customers can apply to Home Uplift for free upgrades. No crawl space rebate is listed, and the federal 25C tax credit no longer covers insulation placed in service after December 31, 2025.

How the TVA EnergyRight Rebates Work

TVA sells power through local utilities, and its EnergyRight program offers home rebates through them, KUB and LCUB included. Four rebates touch insulation work, each $300: attic insulation, wall insulation, envelope air sealing, and duct sealing and insulation. The same TVA rebate page lists HVAC rebates too, such as $800 for a heat pump at 17 SEER2 or higher and $500 for one between 15 and 16.99 SEER2, which matters if you are replacing equipment in the same year.

The catch is who does the work. TVA pays these rebates only when a member of its Quality Contractor Network installs the measure. Before you sign, ask every installer you are considering, us included, whether they are in the network today, and confirm the answer with TVA EnergyRight. A rebate you were counting on is gone if the installer turns out not to qualify.

Amounts can change during the year, so check the TVA page in the same week you sign. For attic insulation and air sealing, it is common to do both on one visit; ask whether both rebates apply to your job.

No Rebate for Crawl Spaces

TVA's list has no floor or crawl space insulation rebate. That surprises people in East Tennessee, where so many homes sit over crawl spaces. Budget for crawl space encapsulation without help: This Old House puts the national range at $1,500 to $15,000. If your ducts run under the floor, ask whether sealing and insulating them can go through the separate duct rebate.

KUB Customers and Home Uplift

KUB's Connect to Savings page sends residential customers to TVA EnergyRight for insulation, HVAC and duct rebates, and to free home energy assessments. KUB does not publish a separate insulation rebate amount of its own, so the TVA figures are the ones to plan around.

Home Uplift is the bigger opportunity for households that qualify. It is a TVA EnergyRight program delivered through KUB that provides free energy upgrades, including insulation, HVAC, water heaters and appliances, to income-qualified customers who own or rent a single family or manufactured home. KUB reports an average value of about $10,000 per home and funds roughly $1 million a year. The KUB home weatherization page explains the program, and applications go through homeuplift.mytva.com/qualify.

If there is any chance you qualify, check Home Uplift before paying for insulation yourself. A free upgrade is worth far more than a $300 rebate.

LCUB Customers

TVA lists Lenoir City Utilities Board as one of its local power companies, with customers in Knox, Loudon and Roane counties, so the EnergyRight rebates should be open to LCUB homes the same way. We did not find a separate LCUB insulation rebate. Call LCUB to confirm before counting on anything beyond the TVA amounts.

Tennessee Weatherization Assistance Program

The Tennessee Housing Development Agency runs the federal Weatherization Assistance Program in the state. It provides free weatherization, including insulation and air sealing, to income-qualified households. We have not confirmed current income limits or waiting times, so ask THDA directly. Like Home Uplift, it is worth checking before you hire anyone.

The Federal 25C Credit Has Ended

The Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit no longer applies to property placed in service after December 31, 2025. A 2025 federal tax law moved its end date up from 2032, so insulation installed in 2026 earns no federal credit.

If your insulation was finished in 2025, you can still claim it on your 2025 return using IRS Form 5695. While it lasted, the credit covered 30 percent of qualified costs with a $1,200 annual cap shared by insulation, air sealing, windows, doors and energy audits, and labor for envelope work did not count. Your tax preparer can tell you how it applies to your return. The IRS credit page has the official wording.

An earlier version of this article said the credit was available for 2026 work. That is no longer true, and we have corrected it. Any quote that still advertises the credit for a 2026 install is out of date.

What $300 Means on a Real Job

The rebates help most on smaller jobs. HomeAdvisor puts attic air sealing at $250 to $750, so the $300 rebate could cover a small sealing job outright or a bit under half of a large one. Against a full attic spray foam project, which This Old House prices at $1,500 to $7,400 nationally, it is a modest share. Pick the insulation that suits the house first, then claim whatever rebates come with it. Documentation Checklist

Keep a folder, paper or digital, with these items for every insulation job:

  • Your power company account details, whether KUB, LCUB or another TVA utility.
  • The installer's Quality Contractor Network status, confirmed with TVA before work starts.
  • An itemized invoice that lists attic insulation, wall insulation, air sealing and duct work as separate lines, with the area and depth or R-value of each.
  • Before and after photos of each area, with a ruler showing the depth.
  • The date the work was finished, since the placed in service date decided 25C eligibility.
  • Copies of every rebate form you send and any confirmation you get back.
  • For work finished in 2025, the receipts and product information your tax preparer needs for Form 5695.

TVA or your utility may ask for more than this. Follow their form first.
