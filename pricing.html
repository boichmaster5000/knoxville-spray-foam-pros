The Short Answer

Expect roughly $1.50 to $3.50 per square foot for installed open cell foam and $3.00 to $5.00 for closed cell, based on HomeGuide's 2026 guide. This Old House puts a typical attic spray foam project at $1,500 to $7,400. Both are national figures checked in October 2026, not a Knoxville Spray Foam Pros price list.

Where your house lands depends on how many inches go on, which foam it is, and how hard the space is to work in. Removal, required coatings and small job minimums add to that.

What a Board Foot Means

A board foot is one square foot of foam, one inch thick. Area times inches equals board feet.

The catch is depth. Open cell gives about R-3.5 to R-4.0 per inch and closed cell about R-6.0 to R-7.4, so open cell needs more inches for the same R-value. At a roof deck, DOE notes that evaluation reports allow 5.5 to 10 inches of open cell, and 5.5 inches lands near R-19 to R-22. Two inches of closed cell lands near R-12 to R-15. A cheap board foot price can still mean an expensive attic.

Before comparing, turn every price into dollars per square foot at a stated depth: board foot price times inches. A square foot price with no inches cannot be checked.

Why the Cost Guides Disagree

  • Some figures are material only. Fixr labels its $0.44 to $0.65 open cell and $1.00 to $1.50 closed cell board foot prices as material. HomeAdvisor and Bob Vila print the same numbers, while Angi's 2026 installed prices run higher. HomeGuide puts closed cell chemical alone at $0.90 to $1.00 per board foot, which leaves little room for labor in a $1.00 installed price.
  • Dates differ. Several pages date from 2024 or early 2025; This Old House and HomeGuide carry 2026 dates.
  • Units differ. Board feet, square feet with no depth, and whole project totals all get mixed together.
  • None is local. They are national blends of large and small jobs. Knoxville labor and the houses here set the real price.

What Moves the Price on Your Job

Thickness. This Old House lists 1 inch of open cell at $0.44 to $1.50 per square foot and 3 inches at $1.30 to $4.50, close to a straight multiple.

Foam type. DOE says open cell expands up to about 150 times its liquid volume and closed cell 35 to 50 times, and it estimates closed cell's installed cost at roughly 60 percent above open cell for equal R-value.

Access. A walk-up attic goes faster than a low-slope attic, a knee wall closet or a tight crawl space. Slow access means more labor hours.

Prep and removal. Old batts or loose fill in the way add removal cost, and HomeAdvisor adds $293 to $875 when drywall has to come off to reach wall cavities.

Job size and minimums. HomeGuide lists minimum job charges of $1,000 to $2,000. Setup costs the same for one rim joist as for a whole attic.

Ignition and thermal barriers. Exposed foam in an attic or crawl space usually needs an ignition barrier. One open cell data sheet calls for a listed intumescent coating of 3 to 10 mils dry in attics, while one closed cell product passed testing that lets it skip the prescriptive ignition barrier there. Foam facing a living space needs a thermal barrier such as 1/2 inch drywall.

Height and staging. Tall shop walls, cathedral ceilings and two-story gable ends need lifts or scaffolding.

Travel. We do not promise to waive travel costs on jobs far from Knoxville. If distance changes the price, the written estimate says so.

Worked Ballpark Examples

These use the Knoxville metro median listing size from Realtor.com data on FRED: 1,986 square feet in September 2026. Listed homes run a little larger than the housing stock as a whole. The rates come from the table, so the results are budgeting brackets, not prices.

Example 1: Topping up a one-story attic floor

On one story, the attic floor is about the living area, 1,986 square feet. Blown-in fiberglass at $1.65 to $2.60 comes to about $3,280 to $5,160. Air sealing first at $250 to $750 makes it about $3,530 to $5,910. TVA lists $300 rebates for attic insulation and for air sealing through its Quality Contractor Network, so ask which apply.

Example 2: Open cell foam on the same roof deck

A sloped deck has more surface than the floor under it, so treat 1,986 square feet as a minimum. At 5.5 inches that is 10,923 board feet. This Old House's 1 inch open cell price of $0.44 to $1.50 per square foot works as a board foot price, so the foam alone runs about $4,810 to $16,380. Removing the old floor insulation at Fixr's $1.50 to $3 adds about $2,980 to $5,960. A spread that wide is why we measure the actual deck.

Example 3: Crawl space under a two-story house

Split over two floors, the footprint is about 993 square feet. Encapsulation at This Old House's $3 to $10 per square foot lands around $2,980 to $9,930. Wall liner height, sealed vents and a dehumidifier push toward the top, and TVA lists no crawl space rebate.

How to Compare Quotes

Line the quotes up and make sure each one answers these points for every area:

  • Thickness in inches and the R-value that depth delivers.
  • Foam type and density: open cell is about 0.5 pound per cubic foot on one data sheet, closed cell about 2.0.
  • Board feet or square feet, with the total written down.
  • Prep and removal: what comes out and how it leaves.
  • Ignition or thermal barrier: whether one is needed and whether it is priced in.
  • Cleanup: masking, trimming and hauling off waste.
  • What is excluded: drywall repair, HVAC changes, dehumidifiers, electrical.
