Quick Answers

What is injection foam?

It is a resin foam mixed at the house and pumped into closed wall cavities. Common types are a tri-polymer resin or a two-part aminoplast resin, both foamed with compressed air. The foam sets within 10 to 60 seconds and does not expand afterward, so it fills the bay by pressure rather than pushing on the drywall.

How much R-value does injection foam add?

One manufacturer's tech sheet lists R-4.59 per inch, so a full 3.5 inch cavity in a 2x4 wall comes to about R-16. A masonry-grade injection foam lists R-4.91 per inch. Those are cavity figures. Studs, plates and headers still carry heat through the wall, so the whole-wall result is lower.

Is injection foam the same as spray foam?

No. Spray foam goes onto open framing and expands as it cures, while injection foam fills enclosed cavities and stays put once set. That is why finished exterior walls get injection foam, and open rim joists, crawl spaces, attics and exposed overhangs get spray foam.

Who Injection Foam Is For

Injection foam is existing wall insulation for houses already finished inside and out. It fits when:

  • Outside walls feel cold to the hand in January, or rooms along them never warm up.
  • A look behind an outlet cover shows an empty bay, or batts slumped to half height.
  • You want insulated walls without tearing out plaster, drywall or siding.
  • The house was built before modern energy codes, as much of the older housing around Knoxville was.

Where it will not work

  • Bays packed with old cellulose, especially the glued-in kind, leave the foam nowhere to flow.
  • Walls with no sheathing behind the siding have nothing to hold the foam.
  • Solid brick laid against brick has no cavity to fill.
  • Thin interior paneling can pop loose under fill pressure.
  • Open bays, rim joists, crawl spaces and attics are not enclosed, so they get spray foam or attic insulation instead.

What it will not fix

Foam fills the cavity, not the framing, so studs still conduct heat. Nor does it replace attic air sealing: Department of Energy guidance puts the most important leaks at the top and bottom of a house.

How Injection Foam Goes Into Each Wall Type

Each wall follows one order: find the bays, open a path into each, fill under pressure, close the hole. The exterior decides where we drill.

Vinyl or aluminum siding

We unhook one course of siding, drill a hole about 2.5 inches across through the sheathing into each bay, and fill from the top down, then from the bottom up. The hole is plugged and the original siding goes back over it.

Brick veneer and stucco

The brick stays put. Holes of 5/8 inch go into mortar joints near the top, middle and bottom of each cavity, then get refilled with mortar, and saved drilling dust helps the patch match. Stucco gets the same three holes per cavity, patched with mortar.

Wood lap, fiber cement and shakes

Sound wood boards come off, the sheathing is drilled and plugged, and the boards go back. Brittle siding is sometimes drilled through and filled, leaving paint touch-up. Fiber cement and shakes are lifted piece by piece, so they take longer.

From the inside

For stone or hard-to-open exteriors we work through the drywall. The room is tented in plastic, holes follow a chalk line, and each is filled, plugged and given a first coat of joint compound. Final sanding and paint are usually yours.

Blocking, fire stops and a full fill

Before filling, the installer runs a probe up and down each bay. Fire blocking, a mid-wall brace or a window header stops it, and the section beyond gets its own hole and fill. Fill is judged by timing, back pressure at the nozzle and, on brick, a small tube above the fill point that moves when foam arrives.

Injection Foam Cost in Knoxville

Price follows wall area and the work of getting in and out. Vinyl opens quickly, brick needs careful mortar work, and fiber cement means handling every board. Second stories, windows, blocking and old insulation all change the labor.

National cost guides do not track injection foam well. For scale only, a Michigan dealer for one injection foam brand reported in October 2025 that all four exterior walls of a house there ran $5,400 to $10,900. That is an out-of-state dealer figure, not a national average and not our price.
