Quick Answers

What R-value does open cell foam give per inch?

About R-3.5 to R-4.0 per inch, depending on the product. The Department of Energy's spray foam guide uses roughly R-3.6, EPA describes about R-3.5, and one manufacturer's data sheet lists R-3.81. That puts a 5.5 inch layer on a roof deck near R-20 and a filled 2x4 stud bay near R-13.

Does open cell foam stop air leaks?

Yes, once it is thick enough. One manufacturer's data sheet shows its open cell foam passing air barrier testing at 3.5 inches, and an evaluation report puts another product at 3.0 inches, so the threshold depends on the foam. Thinner coats still leak some air. Water vapor passes through open cell at any normal depth.

Can open cell go on the roof deck in Knoxville?

Yes. The Department of Energy's spray foam guide lists open cell as acceptable on sloped roof decks and in unvented attics, paired with a vapor-open interior finish in humid climates. Knoxville sits in Climate Zone 4A, and the residential code's extra vapor retarder rule for unvented attic foam only starts in Zone 5.

When Open Cell Makes Sense

Open cell belongs on dry, above-grade framing where you want air sealing and a full cavity of insulation for less than closed cell costs:

  • Upstairs rooms bake every summer because the ductwork runs through a vented attic.
  • You are finishing space under the roof, such as a bonus room, knee wall area or vaulted ceiling.
  • Exterior or interior stud walls are open during a remodel, an addition or new construction.
  • Bedrooms, a home office or a laundry room would benefit from slightly quieter walls.

It is the wrong foam for crawl space walls, basements and other below-grade surfaces, which the Department of Energy guide marks as not acceptable, and for spots exposed to liquid water or the underside of metal roofs. Those jobs call for closed cell spray foam.

How We Install Open Cell Foam

Two liquids meet at the spray gun and swell into a soft foam of about half a pound per cubic foot. Open cell expands up to roughly 150 times its liquid volume, against 35 to 50 times for closed cell, which is why it fills deep rafter bays fast and costs less per inch.

1

Prep and protection

When the roof deck is being sealed, old attic floor insulation usually comes out first. We cover floors, close off HVAC openings and set up exhaust fans, and everyone leaves before spraying starts.

2

Spray in passes

One common data sheet calls for at least 3 inches per pass. We spray to the depth on your estimate, check it with a gauge, and keep foam off flexible ductwork, which that sheet forbids covering.

3

Trim and cover

Foam that rises past wall studs is shaved flush for drywall. Foam facing a living space goes behind a thermal barrier, typically 1/2 inch drywall. A service-only attic needs just an ignition barrier, such as one product's listed intumescent coating at 3 to 10 mils dry.

4

Cure and return

The work area stays under ventilation while the foam cures. One open cell data sheet sets re-occupancy at 24 hours, in line with EPA's summary of manufacturer guidance for two-part foams.

Open Cell Insulation in Knoxville's Climate

A Knoxville roof deck has to get through about 3,528 heating degree days and 1,574 cooling degree days a year in a damp Zone 4A climate. Open cell's vapor openness helps here. One manufacturer measured 6.33 perms at 3.5 inches and 4.03 perms at 5.5 inches, so a deck or wall can still dry if moisture gets in.

Unvented attics follow IRC section R806.5: the attic sits inside the insulated envelope, no Class I vapor retarder goes on the ceiling side, and foam used alone must touch the roof sheathing. If batts or blown insulation go below the foam, the air-impermeable layer against the deck must supply at least R-15 in Zone 4A, roughly 4 inches of qualifying open cell. The Department of Energy notes that allowable open cell depth on a deck runs 5.5 to 10 inches, depending on the product's evaluation report.

Tennessee's statewide ceiling minimum is R-38, but a foamed deck follows a different compliance path, and Maryville moved to the stricter 2024 IECC in 2026. We confirm the target with the local office before settling on a depth.
