Quick Answers

How much does spray foam removal cost?

National guides put most removal at $1.50 to $3 per square foot when machines can do the work, and about $5 per square foot when it has to be chipped out by hand. Fixr and HomeAdvisor both list those figures. Tight spaces and foam bonded hard to sheathing push a job toward the hand rate.

Is it safe to cut out cured spray foam?

Yes, with the right precautions. EPA considers fully cured foam relatively inert, but grinding, sanding, sawing, drilling or heating it can release isocyanates and other emissions. Crews cutting it out wear respiratory protection, avoid heat and open flame, and ventilate the space, and occupants stay out of the work area.

Do you remove old fiberglass and cellulose too?

Yes. We vacuum out loose fill and bag old batts when they are wet, smoke damaged, soiled by rodents, or in the way of roof deck foam. Vermiculite is the exception. EPA guidance says to treat it as possibly containing asbestos, so it should be tested before anyone disturbs it.

When Removal Makes Sense

  • Foam still soft, sticky or crumbly long after spraying, a sign of a wrong mix ratio, wrong temperature or lifts sprayed too thick.
  • A chemical odor that never fades. One forum contributor notes that foam over 10 years old should be well past any smell.
  • A new roof, or a leak hidden behind closed cell on the deck. DOE notes that leaks do not show through that foam and are harder to find.
  • Rodents or insects tunneling through foam or nesting in old attic insulation.
  • Foam over flexible ductwork, which one manufacturer's data sheet forbids, or across the termite inspection gap DOE recommends at rim joists.
  • A switch to roof deck foam, where the old attic floor insulation usually comes out first.

How It Works

Removal is not a one-method job. Each project gets a plan built around the foam type, what it is stuck to, and how much room there is to work.

1

Assess

We look at the foam, the material behind it, and any sign of moisture, rot or mold. When foam may be off-ratio, a lab test before removal can confirm it.

2

Contain and ventilate

The work area is closed off with plastic sheeting and exhausted outdoors. EPA's spraying guidance calls for exhaust about 10 percent greater than supply to hold negative pressure, and we set removal up the same way, with nearby returns sealed.

3

Cut and scrape

Most foam comes out by cutting sections free and scraping off the rest. Forum contributors also mention sanding or blasting by abatement contractors in some cases. Scraping can gouge the surface underneath, so we slow down at sheathing and wiring.

4

Bag, haul and check underneath

Debris is bagged inside the containment and hauled away. Exposed wood gets checked for moisture, rot and mold, and a wet deck has to dry and a leak has to be fixed before anything new goes on, because foam over wet wood traps the water.

Old attic insulation

Loose fiberglass and cellulose leave through a vacuum hose running from the attic to a collection bag outside, so debris is not carried through the house. Batts are rolled and bagged by hand, and droppings and nesting material go with them.

After removal

DOE advises sealing attic air leaks before adding insulation, so we seal top plates and penetrations while the attic is bare. New insulation goes back to at least Tennessee's R-38 minimum, to ENERGY STAR's R-49 to R-60 for Zone 4A, or onto the roof deck as foam.

Why Removal Comes Up So Often Here

Much of the housing around Knoxville is old enough to be on its second or third round of attic insulation. The median home in Oak Ridge was built in 1965 and in Morristown in 1976, so many attics hold insulation that has been through decades of roof repairs and visiting mice.

Wet weather adds to it. Knoxville averages 51.93 inches of rain a year, and a slow roof leak can soak attic insulation without anyone noticing from below.

With roof deck foam, plan before the shingles come off. If a re-roof is coming, bring the roofer and us in together so damaged decking and the foam behind it are handled as one job.
