Quick Answers

How much R-value does closed cell give per inch?

Figure R-6.0 to R-7.4 per inch after aging. The Department of Energy lists typical closed cell at about R-6 to R-6.5, and one manufacturer's November 2024 data sheet shows R-7.4 at 1 inch and R-23 at 3.5 inches. Inch for inch, that is close to double what open cell delivers.

How thick before closed cell blocks air and vapor?

About 1 inch. One manufacturer measured 0.91 perms at that thickness, which qualifies as a Class II vapor retarder (above 0.1 and up to 1.0 perm), and the same inch passed air barrier testing. Most jobs go thicker for R-value, but the moisture and air control are already in place at the first inch.

Where does closed cell beat open cell?

Anywhere the foam may get wet, sits below grade, or has little depth to work with. The Department of Energy lists closed cell as preferred for foundations and below-grade spaces and notes it does not absorb water. That covers crawl space walls, rim joists, basements, steel buildings and 2x4 walls that need more than R-13.

When Closed Cell Is the Right Call

  • You are sealing a crawl space, and the walls need the R-10 continuous or R-13 cavity insulation Tennessee's code sets for Zone 4.
  • Cold air pours in at the band joist, where the Department of Energy's crawl space guide calls for about 1.5 inches of closed cell.
  • Basement or foundation walls sit below grade, where open cell is not an option.
  • A steel roof or wall drips on cool mornings.
  • A 2x4 wall, thin cathedral ceiling or floor over a garage has to reach a high R-value in little depth.
  • You want a flash and batt hybrid: a thin closed cell layer for air and vapor control, with batts or blown insulation filling the rest.

How We Spray Closed Cell

Closed cell cures into a rigid plastic of about 2 pounds per cubic foot, with nearly every cell sealed shut. It expands only about 35 to 50 times its liquid volume, so each inch takes more chemical.

1

Check the surface

Moisture readings and a surface thermometer come first. One manufacturer requires the substrate to be at least 5 degrees F above the dew point, and foam bonds poorly to wet concrete or damp wood, so active leaks get fixed before we spray.

2

Spray in lifts

That same data sheet caps each pass at 2 inches, or one pass up to 3 inches, with a 1 inch minimum. The core has to cool below 100 F before the next pass, because a thick lift traps heat from the curing reaction and can scorch, crack or even catch fire.

3

Measure and cover

We check depth across every area against the estimate, then cover the foam as the space requires (see the FAQ below).

4

Ventilate and re-enter

Exhaust fans run until the foam has cured. Drawing on manufacturer instructions, EPA's guidance points to about a day before people return to the space.

Closed Cell Insulation in Knoxville's Damp Climate

Knoxville averages 51.93 inches of precipitation and a July high of 88.4 F. The Department of Energy's crawl space guide says vented crawl spaces in mixed humid climates tend to gain moisture, which condenses on cool floor framing. Closed cell on the walls and band joist keeps that air off cold surfaces and does not soak up water.

The vapor rules suit closed cell here. In Zone 4 the residential code allows Class II or Class III interior vapor retarders but not Class I, so 1 to 2 inches of closed cell falls in the permitted range without added plastic. Under a roof deck with batts below, IRC Table R806.5 requires at least R-15 of air-impermeable insulation against the sheathing in Zone 4A, which takes about 2 to 2.5 inches of closed cell.

Where termites are active, the Department of Energy's rim joist guidance calls for leaving a 3 inch inspection gap, and we plan the foam line around it.
