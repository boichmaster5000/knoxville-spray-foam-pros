Quick Answers

Is Bearden part of the City of Knoxville?

Yes. Knoxville annexed Bearden in 1962, so building questions go to the city's Plans Review and Inspections office, not to Knox County. The city enforces the 2024 International Residential Code with the 2018 energy code. Bearden lies about 5 miles west of downtown, along Kingston Pike.

Can you insulate plaster walls in an older Bearden house?

Often, but plaster earns a closer look first. Injection foam goes in at low pressure, yet weak lath, old loose fill already in the cavity, or solid brick with no air space can rule it out. We open a few test holes before pricing, and if dense-pack cellulose or leaving the wall alone is smarter, we say so.

What insulation suits a Bearden rental or duplex?

Work done from outside the living space: attic air sealing and blown-in top-ups, crawl space liners and foam on the rim joist. Only 53.7 percent of homes in 37919 are owner occupied, so plenty of owners here are landlords. Those jobs cut drafts and bills without moving tenants out, and you get a written scope to plan around.

What Bearden's Older Housing Means for Insulation

The community was first called Erin, after its Irish settlers, and later took the name of Marcus De Lafayette Bearden, a Union officer and former Knoxville mayor. It grew outward from a fortified house near where Kingston Pike meets Northshore Drive.

Seven decades of building in one ZIP

Of 15,767 housing units in 37919, 55.7 percent were built before 1980. The breakdown: 12.7 percent before 1950, 10.5 percent in the 1950s, 13.8 percent in the 1960s and 18.7 percent in the 1970s. Only 10.6 percent have gone up since 2010. Each era fails differently. Prewar houses may have no wall insulation at all. Postwar ranches often got a few inches in the attic and nothing in the crawl. Seventies houses often have thin batts that have slumped.

Expensive houses beside a big rental market

Just 55.4 percent of units are single-family detached and 1.3 percent are mobile homes, so roughly 43 percent sit in attached or multi-unit buildings. The median owner-occupied value is $526,800. That spread changes the work. On a high-value older home, we protect plaster, trim and hardwood while we drill and patch. On a fourplex or duplex, the priority is shared attics, floors over open crawl spaces and the walls between units.

Sequoyah Hills, at the river's edge

The east edge of the Bearden area, Sequoyah Hills, was platted from the mid-1920s by E. V. Ferrell on a peninsula bounded by the Tennessee River on three sides, with a 10 to 15 foot bluff along its south edge. Its notable homes run from a 1924 Prairie style house through 1930s Tudor Revival to a 1948 Minimal Traditional. Brick, stone and stucco walls need a different approach than frame walls, and river air, with the steam fog local forecasters describe over the water in fall, keeps crawl spaces on the damp side.

Rocky Hill, Bearden Hill and Lakeshore

Rocky Hill, also in 37919, is bounded by Northshore Drive, Wallace Road and Westland Drive. Bearden Hill carries the Knollwood house, and the former Eastern State Hospital grounds on Lyons View Pike are now Lakeshore. Along the corridor, more than 30 shopping centers add up to over three million square feet, so older commercial buildings are part of the picture too.

City codes and the 2024 switch

Knoxville adopted the 2018 residential code on November 20, 2018, then moved to the 2024 editions of the residential, building, plumbing and mechanical codes, plus the 2023 electrical code. The energy code stayed at 2018, which in Tennessee carries the older insulation table: R-38 for ceilings and R-13 for frame walls. Permits run through permits.knoxvilletn.gov and the office at 400 Main Street, Suite 475.
