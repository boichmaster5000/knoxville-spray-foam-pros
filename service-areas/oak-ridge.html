Spray Foam Insulation in Oak Ridge, Tennessee

Oak Ridge is the Manhattan Project town - founded in 1942, built on a single steady curve of cemesto and concrete houses that were supposed to be temporary. Eighty years later most of those houses are still standing, still occupied, and still bleeding heat. Add the 1950s-1960s ranches in Highland View, Woodland, and West Hills plus the newer subdivisions out toward Karns Quarry, and Oak Ridge is a community where spray foam insulation has more measurable upside than almost anywhere in East Tennessee. Quick Answers

Can you insulate an original cemesto house?

Yes, from the attic and the crawl space, and carefully. The Atomic Heritage Foundation describes cemesto panels as cement and asbestos board, so we never drill, cut or sand them without a lab test first. Air sealing the ceiling, deepening the attic and sealing the crawl space or rim joist all leave the panels alone.

Who handles building permits in Oak Ridge?

The city, not Anderson County. Oak Ridge Planning and Development runs a Codes Enforcement division at 200 South Tulane Avenue, with permits through its CityView portal. The city's codes page lists the 2024 residential code with the 2018 energy code and its 2009 insulation chart. We confirm with that office before quoting.

Why do Oak Ridge crawl spaces stay so damp?

Rain, mostly, plus terrain. Oak Ridge averages about 59.7 inches of precipitation a year, well above Knoxville's 52, and many streets sit in valleys between long parallel ridges inside a bend of the Clinch River. We check grading and gutters before sealing a crawl, then add a liner, wall foam and drying.

Oak Ridge Housing, From Alphabet Houses to Rarity Ridge

The federal government took about 59,000 acres in 1942 for the Manhattan Project and built a city from nothing; by 1945 its population was near 75,000. Oak Ridge incorporated in 1959, spans Anderson and Roane counties, and counted 31,402 residents in 2020.

A 1965 median, and a third of homes from the 1940s

The median Oak Ridge home was built in 1965. Of 15,279 housing units, 67.3 percent predate 1980, and 31.2 percent went up in the 1940s alone, against about 1 percent before 1940. That leaves roughly 35 percent built from 1950 through 1979. They are a different job from the wartime houses: framed walls that can usually take injection foam, low attics, and crawl spaces with little or nothing on the walls.

Cemesto and flat-top houses

Roughly 3,000 cemesto homes, made with cement and asbestos board panels and lettered by type, went up first. Later prefabricated houses arrived in sections, and 30 to 40 homes a day were handed to their occupants. Skidmore, Owings and Merrill designed the alphabet houses: types A through H were cemesto, and a separate set of flat-top designs carried the letters A, B, C, S and V. Many are still private homes, and a Type A flat-top sits at the Children's Museum of Oak Ridge. A flat-top has no attic to fill, so its roof insulation waits for a new roof.

Value, ownership and the labs

About 69.1 percent of homes are single-family detached, 0.4 percent are mobile homes and 65.8 percent are owner occupied, with a median owner-occupied value of $270,200. At that value, comfort per dollar matters, which often favors air sealing and blown-in insulation over a whole-house foam job. The Department of Energy, Oak Ridge National Laboratory and the Y-12 National Security Complex anchor the economy, and Y-12 alone employs more than 7,000 people.

Five ridges, two lakes, heavy rain

Blackoak, East Fork, Pine, Chestnut and Haw ridges run parallel through the city inside the Clinch River's bend, with Melton Hill Lake to the east and Watts Bar Lake to the south. Elevation climbs from about 850 feet to 1,356 at Melton Hill. With 59.7 inches of yearly precipitation, foundations on valley floors see a lot of runoff.

City codes, Anderson County beyond

Inside city limits, Codes Enforcement handles permits and inspections. Its page lists the 2024 residential and building codes, the 2023 electrical code, and the 2018 energy code with the 2009 chart, which sets an R-38 ceiling and R-13 frame walls. Outside the city, Anderson County's Office of Planning and Development at 100 N Main Street in Clinton covers building and codes, but its page publishes no code edition, so we call first.

Neighborhoods old and new

Neighborhoods include Scarboro, Robertsville, Elza, Wheat and Hendrix Creek. Rarity Ridge, inside The Preserve at Oak Ridge, is newer construction, where an upgrade is cheapest before drywall.

Most-Requested Services in Oak Ridge

  • Attic insulation: in gabled cemestos and mid-century ranches, the attic is where we add the most without touching an original wall.
  • Crawl space vapor barriers: nearly 60 inches of yearly precipitation keeps the ground under Oak Ridge houses wet.
  • Rim joist insulation: the sill joint of a 1940s or 1950s house is usually one of its leakiest seams.
  • Injection foam: suited to the framed walls of the 1950s through 1970s houses, never to cemesto panels.
  • Insulation removal: eighty-year-old attic fill is often dirty or compressed, and anything that might be vermiculite gets tested before it moves.
  • New construction insulation: Rarity Ridge and other new subdivisions can beat the code minimum while walls are open.
