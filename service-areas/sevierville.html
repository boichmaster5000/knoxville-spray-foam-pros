Quick Answers

Can you insulate a Sevierville rental cabin without losing bookings?

Mostly, with some calendar work. Two-part spray foam means nobody stays in the cabin during the job or for about 24 hours after, per EPA's summary of manufacturer guidance, so block one extra night past the work days. Blown-in and batt work rarely need an overnight gap. We map the job against your open dates at the estimate.

Which code covers new homes in Sevierville?

Inside city limits, Ordinance O-2023-021 puts houses under the 2018 International Residential Code and an amended 2018 IECC, with 2021 editions for commercial and most trade codes. Land outside the city goes through Sevier County Building Inspections on Cedar Street, which keeps its own adoption. We confirm which office has your parcel before pricing new work.

How old is Sevierville's housing?

Young for East Tennessee. The median year built is 1994, and only about a quarter of the city's 9,631 units predate 1980. The busiest decades were the 2000s with 1,841 units, the 1990s with 1,779 and the 1980s with 1,670. That makes attics and crawls more common jobs here than empty walls.

What Is Different About Sevierville Houses

Fewer plain detached houses. Only about 46 percent of Sevierville's housing units are single-family detached, a low share that reflects condos, cabins and multi-unit tourist lodging. Another 6.4 percent are mobile homes. Shared walls, owners' associations and units stacked over one another change the job: the work may go one unit at a time, and noise between units can matter as much as heat.

A lot of buildings sit empty part of the time. The 2020 census counted 20.4 percent of Sevierville housing units as vacant, with a 7.7 percent rental vacancy rate, a sign of how much seasonal and overnight rental property the city holds. A closed-up building with the thermostat set back gives crawl space humidity time to build where nobody is looking.

Hot summers, real winters. At about 971 feet, Sevierville's 1991 to 2020 normals show July highs averaging 89.6 F with nights near 67.1 F, about 48.78 inches of precipitation, and 3.6 inches of snow a year. The record range runs from 106 F down to 24 below zero. Sevier County sits in Climate Zone 4A, and a cabin attic or a vaulted ceiling here has to handle both ends of that range.

Steady new construction. The city has added 1,916 housing units since 2010. Kodak, north of town near I-40 exit 407, is the most active new-home area, and subdivisions such as Robertson Farm near the hospital and Savannah Glen with its one-level homes are still building. A new house is the easiest time to get insulation right, because every cavity is still open.

Two permit offices and a busy commercial strip. City permits go through Development and Code Enforcement at 120 Gary Wade Boulevard; county parcels go to the county office. Along Dolly Parton Parkway, a 3 square mile Tourism Development Zone created in 2004 anchors the outlets and retail, and commercial buildings inside the city fall under its 2021 commercial codes.

Most-Requested Services in Sevierville
