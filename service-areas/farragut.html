Quick Answers

Do newer Farragut homes need more insulation?

Usually at the attic, yes. A house finished in the 1990s went up long before Tennessee's current energy code rule took effect in July 2020, and ENERGY STAR now suggests R-49 to R-60 for a Zone 4A attic. We measure the depth you have, look at the air sealing under it, and say plainly whether a top-up pays.

Who handles building permits in Farragut?

The town does, not Knox County. Farragut's Community Development department runs a Planning and Codes division at 11408 Municipal Center Drive, and permits are filed through the MGO Connect portal. Whether an insulation job needs one depends on its scope, so we ask the town when work touches framing, an addition or a basement finish.

Is spray foam worth it in a Farragut attic?

It is when the attic holds the ductwork or air handler, or when a bonus room is tucked under the roof. Foam on the underside of the roof deck pulls that equipment inside the insulated shell. If the attic floor is open and the ducts run below it, air sealing plus blown-in insulation usually costs less.

Farragut Housing, Terrain and Codes

Farragut is its own town with its own building office, and 70.6 percent of its housing went up in 1980 or later.

A 1992 median, built in three busy decades

Of the town's 9,261 housing units, 22.8 percent date to the 1990s, 15.5 percent to the 2000s and 14.5 percent to the 2010s. Under 2 percent were built before 1950. A wall framed in those decades normally got batts during construction, so we check it, but injection foam is seldom the first job here. The weak spots sit higher: attic insulation that has settled or runs thin at the eaves, unsealed top plates, and recessed lights that leak into the attic.

Detached, owner-occupied and valuable

About 88.3 percent of homes are single-family detached, the census counts no mobile homes, and 84.7 percent are owner occupied, with a median owner-occupied value of $583,400. Owners planning to stay usually ask for comfort over the cheapest pass: an even temperature upstairs and a garage that works as a shop.

Between Blackoak Ridge and Fort Loudoun Lake

The town covers 16.14 square miles of hilly ground at about 958 feet, with Blackoak Ridge to the north and the Tennessee River, here Fort Loudoun Lake, to the south. Area humidity averages 71.5 percent and climbs near 76 percent in August and September, and WATE counts more than 40 fog days a year, with steam fog over the lakes in fall. On hillside lots near the water, crawl spaces and block foundation walls stay damp, so we test wood moisture before any foam goes on.

The older pockets

Not everything is new. Some 29.4 percent of units predate 1980, and Campbell's Station Inn, a brick inn from 1810, still stands on Kingston Pike by Town Hall. Next door, Concord village dates to 1854 and lost about a third of its land when Fort Loudoun Lake filled by 1944. In a house of that age we pull an outlet cover or drill a test hole before quoting, because an empty wall changes the plan.

Town codes since 1980

Residents voted to incorporate on January 16, 1980, largely to keep Knoxville from annexing the Kingston Pike corridor. The Board of Mayor and Aldermen adopted the 2018 International Building Code in April 2019 and the 2018 residential and energy codes later that year. We found no newer adoption, so we confirm with the town. Under Tennessee's amended table the attic minimum is R-38 and a framed wall R-13, and we treat those as a floor, not a goal.

Most-Requested Services in Farragut

  • Attic insulation: the 1990s and 2000s attics that make up so much of the town are the first place we measure against the R-49 to R-60 ENERGY STAR range.
  • Open cell spray foam: where the air handler and ducts live in the attic, foam under the roof deck brings them inside the conditioned space.
  • Garage and bonus room insulation: the room over a two-car garage has knee walls, a cold floor and a sloped ceiling, all touching unconditioned space.
  • Air sealing: recessed lights, attic hatches and plumbing chases in newer homes leak house air upward, and sealing them comes before any new insulation.
  • Crawl space vapor barriers: lots that slope toward the lake keep crawl spaces wet in late summer, when humidity here peaks near 76 percent.
  • Rim joist insulation: walk-out lower levels on hillside lots leave a long band joist exposed above the foundation.
