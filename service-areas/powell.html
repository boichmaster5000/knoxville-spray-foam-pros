Quick Answers

Do manufactured homes in Powell take insulation?

Yes, though the options are narrower than on a site-built house. Most of the gain comes from underneath: repairing the belly wrap, refilling the floor cavity and sealing plumbing holes. Skirting can cut the wind under the home. The roof cavity is usually shallow, which limits how much can be added up top.

Who handles permits for a Powell address?

Usually Knox County Codes Administration and Inspection, through the Knox Permit Portal, since Powell has no town government. A few parcels differ: the old Powell STOLport airstrip at I-75 and Emory Road was annexed by Knoxville and approved for a shopping center in 2013. We confirm the jurisdiction by address before any permitted work.

How old are houses in Powell?

Mixed, with a lean toward the 2000s. The 37849 ZIP has a median build year of 1993, and 32.4 percent of its 11,545 housing units predate 1980. The biggest single decade was 2000 to 2009, at 26.0 percent. Older homes need wall and crawl space checks; the 2000s homes mostly need attic work.

Local Conditions in Powell

Settled in 1789, Powell takes its name from the rail stop that honored resident Columbus Powell. The census-designated place counted 13,802 people and 5,766 housing units in 2020, with 4.7 percent vacant, and the larger 37849 ZIP holds about 29,375 residents. Around 82.4 percent of homes in the ZIP are owner occupied and 73.4 percent are single-family detached.

Between Copper Ridge and Beaver Ridge

Powell lies in the Beaver Creek Valley at about 1,060 feet, along Emory Road, with I-75 on the east, Clinton Highway on the west and Knoxville across Beaver Ridge to the southeast. A creek valley pinned between two ridges is classic ground for the radiation fog that WATE says shows up on more than 40 days a year around Knoxville. Damp, still air settles under houses, which is hard on crawl spaces and on the bellies of manufactured homes.

Brick country

Early Powell brickmakers dug their clay from Beaver Creek, and brick was a pillar of the local economy. The old depot still stands downtown, and the Airplane Service Station is on the National Register. On an older brick-faced house, wall insulation usually means injection through mortar joints, if there is a framed cavity behind the brick.

Manufactured homes: what fits and what does not

At 10.4 percent of the 37849 housing stock, manufactured homes make up a larger share here than in Farragut, Bearden, Karns or Oak Ridge. They are built differently from site-built houses, so the insulation menu is different too.

County code, with optional ventilation

Knox County adopted the 2018 codes for permits from January 1, 2019, kept the older energy R-values in its amendments, and made whole-house mechanical ventilation optional. A 2023 county newsletter was still discussing amendments to the 2018 residential code. Optional ventilation matters when we tighten an older Powell house: DOE says to confirm fresh air and combustion appliance venting before air sealing, and we do.

Most-Requested Services in Powell

  • Crawl space vapor barriers: valley-floor fog and creek moisture make a sealed ground liner the base of most crawl space fixes here.
  • Crawl space insulation: the pre-1980 third of Powell's housing often sits over a crawl with nothing on its walls.
  • Blown-in insulation: the low-cost way to deepen attics over the 2000s subdivisions, and sometimes a manufactured home's floor cavity.
  • Air sealing: older houses here leak at top plates and chases, and the county's optional-ventilation rule means we plan fresh air along with it.
  • Injection foam: older brick and frame walls with an empty cavity can be filled through mortar joints or behind siding.
  • Rim joist insulation: the band joist above a damp crawl is cold and drafty, and foam there seals and insulates at once.

Powell Insulation FAQ

Start with the belly. We open damaged sections, pull out wet or fallen insulation, fix any plumbing drips, refill the floor cavity and patch the bottom board with new material. Sealing holes around pipes and ducts stops drafts at the same time. Skirting helps by blocking wind, but it is a shield, not insulation, and the ground under the home still needs to stay dry.
