Why Powell Homes Need Crawl Space Encapsulation

Powell's housing stock - the bulk of which was built between 1965 and 1995 - sits almost universally on crawl space foundations. Every one of those crawls was originally built with foundation vents (per the pre-2008 code) and uninsulated rim joists. Three decades of East Tennessee humidity have done predictable damage: original fiberglass batts have sagged out of the floor joist bays and now sit on the dirt floor soaking up ground moisture. Wood framing has cycled through moisture content swings that show as cupped subfloor and creaky joists. Cold floors in winter are the universal complaint.

Crawl space encapsulation - sealing the vents, installing a 20-mil reinforced vapor liner, spraying 2 inches of closed cell on the rim and foundation walls, and adding a small dehumidifier - solves all of it. Powell homeowners who encapsulate routinely report floor temperatures up 10-15°F in winter and HVAC bills down $40-$80 a month year-round. We do these projects in Powell weekly, typical scope and cost in the $5,500-$10,500 range depending on crawl size.
