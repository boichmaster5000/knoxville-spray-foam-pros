Quick Answers

How old are the houses in West Knoxville?

That depends on the ZIP. Census estimates put the median build year at 1977 in 37919, 1985 in 37923, 1998 in 37922 and 2004 in 37932, where 38.5 percent of homes went up in 2010 or later. The older ZIPs raise wall and crawl space questions; the newer ones raise attic and bonus room questions.

Is West Knoxville under city or county codes?

Mostly city. Addresses inside Knoxville limits go through Plans Review and Inspections at 400 Main Street, which enforces the 2024 residential code with the 2018 energy code. Hardin Valley and other unincorporated pieces of western Knox County answer to Knox County Codes instead, so we confirm the jurisdiction for each address.

Can condo and townhouse owners here add insulation?

Often, within limits. In 37923 only 49.8 percent of homes are single-family detached, so many owners share walls, roofs and attics. Air sealing, attic top-ups over your own unit and sound insulation at a party wall are usually workable. Anything on the roof or exterior needs the association's approval before we schedule it.

West Hills ranches

West Hills, about 7 miles west of downtown and across from West Town Mall, was Knoxville's first big post-war subdivision and its first mostly ranch-style neighborhood, developed from the 1950s by Morgan Schubert, with a 1954 Parade of Homes on Stockton Drive. A long, low ranch means a wide attic floor and a long foundation perimeter, so the usual plan is attic air sealing with a top-up overhead and foam on the band joist below.

Hardin Valley growth

Hardin Valley, about 15.5 miles west of downtown in unincorporated Knox County, has been called the fastest growing part of the county since the late 2000s, with more than 1,200 building permits issued after July 2014, mostly for subdivisions and apartments. Pellissippi State's main campus and Hardin Valley Academy, opened in 2008, sit there. New homes meet code, but in Tennessee code still means an R-38 attic and R-13 walls. Buyers who want more should raise it before the walls close.

Ridges, river and summer heat

Knoxville lies in the Great Appalachian Valley, with the riverfront near 800 feet and West Knoxville hilltops a little over 1,000. The city averages about 36 days a year at 90 F or hotter, and a vented attic runs far hotter than the air outside, which is rough on ductwork routed through it.
