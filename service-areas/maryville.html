Quick Answers

Which insulation code applies to a Maryville address?

Inside city limits it is the 2024 IECC with Maryville's local modifications, in force since January 1, 2026. Homes outside the city answer to Blount County Development Services, which lists the 2018 ICC codes. Most of Tennessee builds to the amended 2018 IECC, whose Zone 4 table calls for an R-38 ceiling and R-13 walls.

Open cell or closed cell insulation for a Maryville home?

Usually some of each. Open cell insulation tends to make sense in Maryville attics and under roof decks, at roughly R-3.6 to R-4 per inch. Closed cell insulation runs about R-6 to R-7.4 per inch, and a single inch already acts as a Class II vapor retarder, so it goes on crawl space walls, rim joists and metal.

How old is the typical Maryville house?

The median home in Maryville dates to 1985, per the Census Bureau's 2024 five-year survey. That middle number hides a wide spread: 44 percent of the city's 12,901 housing units were built before 1980, 14 percent before 1950, and close to one in five between 2010 and 2019.

What Shapes Insulation Work in Maryville

A newer code than the rest of the area

The city adopted its 2024 package on December 2, 2025, keeping only the 2017 electrical code. Permits run through Development Services in the Municipal Building at 416 W. Broadway Avenue, filed on the city's iWorQ portal. For a new house or an addition inside the city, the old R-38 ceiling and R-13 wall figures no longer set the floor. For direction, the 2021 model energy code, one edition before Maryville's, had already raised the Zone 4 ceiling to R-60. Maryville also publishes local changes to the 2024 IECC, so we confirm required values with the codes office before pricing new work.

Houses from every decade

Census figures put 14.0 percent of Maryville's housing before 1950 and another 10.9 percent in the 1950s, while 18.5 percent went up between 2010 and 2019. In a house from the 1940s or 1950s, we first find out whether the exterior walls hold anything and how the floor framing over the crawl looks. In a house from the 2010s, the attic is where the upside usually sits: the state table asks for R-38 in a Zone 4 ceiling, while ENERGY STAR recommends R-49 to R-60 for a Zone 4A attic.

Owners, renters and home values

About 73 percent of Maryville homes are detached single-family houses, 67.5 percent are owner occupied, and the median owner-occupied value is $358,100. That leaves roughly a third of households renting. Landlords should plan two-part spray foam around a vacancy, because EPA notes that manufacturers commonly advise about 24 hours before anyone moves back in.

Foothills weather and damp crawl spaces

Maryville sits at about 981 feet in the Great Smoky Mountain foothills, with Chilhowee Mountain rising to the south and the Little River close by. July runs from about 69 F at night to 87 F in the afternoon, and January from 29 F to 46 F, so Blount County sits in Climate Zone 4A with a real season at each end. Department of Energy research found that vented crawl spaces in humid climates often draw in outdoor air wetter than the crawl itself, so the crawl gets a close look on every older Maryville home we walk.

City line or county line

Outlying communities such as Wildwood, Ellejoy and Clover Hill ring the city on the east and west. A house outside the city line goes through Blount County Development Services and its 2018 codes, so before quoting code R-values for new work, we confirm which side of the line the lot is on.
