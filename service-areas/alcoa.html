Quick Answers

Do you do crawl space encapsulation in Alcoa?

Yes. A typical Alcoa encapsulation means a continuous Class I liner over the ground with seams lapped and sealed, the vents closed, foam on the foundation walls and rim joist, and a way to dry the air, such as a dehumidifier. We check drainage and wood moisture first, because a liner will not fix standing water.

What does crawl space encapsulation cost in Alcoa?

This Old House's March 2026 national guide puts encapsulation between $1,500 and $15,000, or roughly $3 to $10 per square foot, with about $5,500 as the average. Those are national figures, not our prices. Crawl height, standing water, and whether the walls get foam move the number most. A free estimate gives the real one.

How old are Alcoa's houses?

The median Alcoa home was built in 1980. Just over half of the city's 5,641 housing units predate that year, including 13.0 percent built before 1940 and 10.2 percent from the 1940s, which covers much of the original company stock. On the newer side, about one unit in five went up in 2010 or later.

Alcoa Homes, Ground and Code

A company town that is now a century old

The aluminum company had 150 employee houses standing by 1914 and 700 by 1920, spread across four planned sections: Vose and Springbrook to the north, Bassel and Hall to the south. In the early 1950s it began selling those houses to the people living in them. That history explains what we tend to find. The houses went up generations before anyone wrote an energy code, and seventy years of separate owners means each one has its own patchwork of upgrades, from a newer attic to walls nobody has opened.

Crawl spaces carry the most risk

Alcoa averages about 47.5 inches of precipitation a year, and the Little River runs along the east side of the city at around 932 feet of elevation. Wet ground and humid summer air meet under the floor. The Department of Energy found that in humid climates, air pulled in through foundation vents is often wetter than the crawl it enters, and that sealed, conditioned crawl spaces used 15 to 18 percent less heating and cooling energy than vented ones while cutting crawl humidity by more than 20 percent. Its crawl space guide aims for framing below 17 percent moisture content and air between 30 and 50 percent relative humidity, with a ground liner of at least 10 mil poly or a cross-laminated 6 mil sheet.

Old and new side by side

About 71.8 percent of Alcoa homes are detached houses, 5.5 percent are mobile homes, and 66.6 percent are owner occupied, with a median owner-occupied value of $277,100. Next to the 1910s and 1940s stock sits the fifth of the city built since 2010. Those newer walls went in insulated, so on them the attic is where we look first.

The permit office and its code

City of Alcoa Building and Codes Enforcement, on Associates Boulevard, enforces the 2018 IRC and 2018 IECC with local amendments, takes permits through an online portal, and schedules inspections by appointment with a day of notice. That is a different code cycle from Maryville next door. Under the Tennessee version of the 2018 IECC, a Zone 4 crawl space wall calls for R-10 continuous or R-13 cavity insulation.

Living next to the airport

McGhee Tyson Airport sits inside Alcoa, hosts the 134th Air Refueling Wing of the Tennessee Air National Guard, and handled a record 3.63 million passengers in 2025. If aircraft noise is what bothers you most, raise it at the estimate and we will tell you plainly what insulation can and cannot do about it.

Most-Requested Services in Alcoa

  • Crawl space vapor barriers and encapsulation: the core of most crawl space work in Alcoa, sized to code at 70 pints of dehumidification a day per 1,000 square feet of crawl floor when a dehumidifier is the drying method.
  • Crawl space insulation: once the vents are shut, the foundation walls become the thermal boundary, so we foam them instead of stuffing batts between the floor joists.
  • Rim joist insulation: the band where an old company house meets its foundation is one of the leakiest joints in any home, and it is easy to reach from the crawl.
  • Injection foam for existing walls: a fit for pre-1940 houses with sheathing and closed cavities, though not for walls with no sheathing behind the siding or cavities already packed with old cellulose.
  • Attic insulation: the post-2010 homes and the remodeled older ones alike tend to have the most room to improve overhead.
  • Soundproofing insulation: for bedrooms near the airport, with an honest caveat that cavity insulation adds only a few STC points and the big gains come from extra drywall and decoupled framing.
